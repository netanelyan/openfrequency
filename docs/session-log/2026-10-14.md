## Done

- **The content model.** Ten kinds of post (story, what-happened, on-this-day,
  guess-who, head-to-head, ranking, iceberg, hot-take, myth-fact, meme), each a
  shape checked in code and drawn by shared parts in every template: versus
  cards, rank numbers, an iceberg's depth, clue badges, myth and fact stamps, meme
  cards, timelines, the call to action and the keywords on the last slide.
  Two new templates (`card`, `corner`). Two captions per post. The code
  picks the cover pattern and the call to action; results are kept per pattern; the
  mix of entertaining and number posts moves with the results within 40% to 80%.
- **The three accounts**, written in the private folder from researched sources:
  four research tasks ran beside the engine work, one per account and one on the
  platforms' delete endpoints, and every source was read through the engine's own
  network door with its terms checked. The two old accounts are archived.
- **Takedowns** (Instagram deleted through the Graph API where Meta allows it,
  TikTok by hand, a second notice pauses the account), **the image screen**
  (words, red, the model, a person), **the fight week** (a calendar from
  Wikipedia, the tale of the tape, the pick, the recap only once the card is over).
- **Sources**: Wikipedia and Wikidata through their APIs (sections through the
  REST HTML, with tables), a day filter for dated lists, Latin-1 pages, JSON
  `where` and `last`, a User-Agent with a contact, a wait on 429.
- **The demo pack** has a format of every kind and builds all of them offline; a
  subagent wrote its fictional documents and drafts.
- **The launch set** (`npm run launch`): 9 posts per account, drafted by Claude
  Code subagents on the engine's exact prompts, built by the real pipeline,
  reviewed blind by another subagent.

## Found and fixed on the way

The demo content found five engine bugs: the on-this-day date check read the
year as the month (no on-this-day post could ever be built); a document with no
recording used up one of the six documents a format may try; a top 10 could never
pass, its five lines counted as five sentences; the 320-character caption limit
made the 200-character TikTok caption impossible; Hebrew number words on the
cover went unchecked. The launch drafts found four more: "monday.com" and
"Wix.com" refused as web addresses; a guess-who's answer ("Fiverr") refused as
"mostly not Hebrew"; a shape error that named the wrong slide; and the two
documents of a head-to-head numbered in a different order by the drafting tool
and by the engine, which made a correct fact fail. Recording the launch documents
hit Wikipedia's rate limit (the door now waits). A first launch build put a cover
photo full of crests and logos on a sports post: that account draws its own
covers now. A web address check, a language check and four prompt sentences
changed; each fix has a test.

## The launch set, three rounds

27 posts, 9 per account, each one built by the real pipeline for TikTok and
Instagram and reviewed blind by a fresh subagent playing a skeptical Israeli
viewer. Round 2 carried each post's round-1 review as notes; round 3 did again.
The blind bar was strict (stop the scroll, swipe to the end, a reason to save,
send or comment), and the verdicts were 2, 3 and 3 of 27, swinging on the same
post from round to round. They are kept with each post for the owner, who decides.

The reviews were worth more for the engine than for the scores. They found, and
each now has a fix and a test:
- a guess-who's source line naming its answer;
- English titles and number ranges reversed inside Hebrew lines;
- a display face whose 7 reads as a Hebrew letter;
- a cut last line the fit check passed;
- a keyword row read as SEO;
- hashtags matched inside other words;
- a question asked twice at a caption's end;
- a number printed twice;
- a stripe through the text;
- labels that jumped from slide to slide;
- photos of a rock, a plant and holly berries found for fighters' names;
- a credit that named "Unknown author" twice.

The engine's own second look, which a recorded build cannot take, now runs in
the launch harness too. It showed that the review took a myth for the post's
claim, and that it could not see a claim that mistranslated its quote; both are
fixed. Two packs' rules were wrong for their sport ("נעצר" is a stoppage before
it is an arrest), and the list check read the comma inside a score as a list.

## Measured, and the forecast moved

With the review and the rewrite measured instead of estimated, a post costs $0.023
to $0.039 batched and cached, and a published post, counting the redrafts and the
stopped posts, $0.040 to $0.068. At 180 posts that is $7.20 to $12.20, inside $15,
up from the $3.80 to $6.70 estimated earlier today. The worst cases no longer fit,
and costs.md says so. The cap still holds, and the planner publishes fewer posts.
